0. ABOUT THIS DOCUMENT

One Voice is a student research prototype built for VT Hacks. It is not a
commercial product, it is not offered as a service, and no one is charged for
it.

This document is written to be checkable. Every statement about current
behaviour describes something present in the source code, and the code is
available for anyone who wants to verify it. Features that are planned but not
finished are confined to section 13 and labelled as planned. We would rather
under-claim than describe something that does not exist.

THIS IS NOT LEGAL ADVICE. This policy was written by the project team to
describe the software honestly. It has not been reviewed by a lawyer and is
not a substitute for legal advice about your own use of the software,
particularly regarding recording consent and biometric data in your
jurisdiction. See sections 6 and 10.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE SHORT VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * Speech separation, face tracking and transcription all run on your own
    computer. There is no One Voice server.
  * We have no accounts, no analytics, no cookies, no tracking, no ads.
  * We never receive your audio, video, face data or transcripts.
  * Camera and microphone open only after you press "Start listening", and
    stop when you press Stop.
  * Face matching produces biometric data. It is held in memory only, never
    written to disk, and discarded when the session ends. See section 6.
  * A visible on-screen recording indicator is shown the entire time a clip is
    being recorded. Recording never happens silently.
  * Nothing is written to disk unless you start a recording.
  * One planned feature (section 13.4) would send images to a third-party
    cloud service. It is off by default and requires explicit consent.
  * One Voice captures other people. Getting their consent is your
    responsibility, not the software's. See section 10.

3. THE LANDING WEBSITE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The One Voice website is a static page served from your own machine on
127.0.0.1 (localhost). It is not published on the internet.

  * It does not request camera or microphone access.
  * It does not set cookies or use local storage for tracking.
  * It contains no analytics, tag managers, pixels or third-party scripts.
  * It makes no network requests. All code and assets, including the 3D
    library, are bundled locally.
  * The "Connect One Voice" button opens an on-page dialog. It is a
    placeholder and sends nothing anywhere.

The cafe comparison shown on the site is a visual illustration, not model
output, and not a recording of real people made by this software.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHAT THE APPLICATION CAPTURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

When you start a listening session, the application accesses:

  Camera     Video frames of whoever is in front of the lens, used to detect
             faces, track them between frames, and read mouth movement to
             guide separation.

  Microphone Audio from the room, which necessarily includes every voice
             within range, not only the person you select.

Both open only after an explicit Start action, and both are released when you
press Stop or close the session. Closing a browser tab alone does not stop
capture; the session must be stopped.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHERE PROCESSING HAPPENS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Speech separation, face detection, face matching, noise suppression and
transcription all run locally, on the laptop the camera and microphone are
connected to.

No audio, video, images, face data or transcripts are uploaded to any server
by any feature currently built. The single planned exception is described in
section 13.4 and is not enabled by default.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. BIOMETRIC DATA - FACE MATCHING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This section exists because face matching is the most regulated thing One
Voice does, and it deserves to be described plainly rather than buried.

WHAT HAPPENS

To keep a speaker's label attached to them when they turn their head or are
briefly hidden, the application computes a numerical representation of each
detected face - a face embedding. Depending on jurisdiction, a face embedding
may be classified as biometric data, biometric identifiers, or special
category personal data.

HOW IT IS HANDLED

  * Embeddings are held in memory only. They are never written to disk.
  * They are never transmitted anywhere, and no API receives them.
  * At most three anonymous profiles exist at a time, labelled "Person 1",
    "Person 2", "Person 3".
  * They are discarded when the session ends. Nothing persists between
    sessions, and the software cannot recognise the same person tomorrow.
  * There is no face database, no enrolment step and no link to any name,
    account or identity. The software does not know who anyone is.
  * No face images or crops are saved.

WHAT THIS MEANS LEGALLY

We are not lawyers and this is not legal advice. But you should be aware that:

  * Illinois' Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA) regulates the
    collection of face geometry and allows individuals to sue directly.
    Texas (CUBI) and Washington have comparable laws.
  * Under the EU/UK GDPR, biometric data processed for the purpose of uniquely
    identifying a person is special category data under Article 9 and requires
    a specific lawful basis, usually explicit consent.
  * Several of these regimes turn on notice and consent before collection -
    not on whether the data was stored afterwards.

One Voice is designed to minimise this exposure: transient, local, anonymous,
never stored, never transmitted. That is a genuinely strong position, but it is
not a legal exemption. If you deploy One Voice anywhere other than personal
experimentation - a workplace, a public venue, a study, a product - take
proper advice first and obtain informed consent from the people in frame.

7. RECORDING, AND THE RECORDING INDICATOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recording is separate from listening. Running a session records nothing.

While a recording is active, a clearly visible recording indicator is shown on
screen for the entire duration, together with an elapsed timer. It cannot be
hidden while recording continues. One Voice does not record silently and is not
designed to be used covertly.

When you start a recording, the application writes to a folder on your own
computer:

  * The captured audio for that conversation
  * The isolated audio that was sent to playback
  * A small file recording timing and status information for the clip

These files stay on your machine. They are not uploaded and are not shared.

WHAT THE SAVED AUDIO ACTUALLY CONTAINS

The captured track is the room as the microphone heard it, so it may include
other voices that were audible at the time, not only the person you selected.
The isolated track contains the selected speaker. Treat a saved recording as a
recording of the whole situation you were in, and share it accordingly.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. RETENTION AND DELETION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Face embeddings          In memory only. Discarded when the session ends.
                           Never written to disk.

  Recorded conversations   Kept on your computer until you delete them. The
                           software never expires or cleans them up, and never
                           needs them again after the clip finishes.

  Transcripts, summaries   Stored alongside the conversation they belong to,
  (when built, see 13)     on your machine, and deleted with it.

  Downloaded model files   Cached on your computer so they are not fetched
                           again. Contain no personal data.

  Anything sent under      Held by the third-party provider under their
  section 13.4             retention policy, not ours. We cannot retrieve or
                           delete it.

To remove everything One Voice has stored locally, delete the saved
conversation folders and the cached model files. Nothing else is written.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. NETWORK CONNECTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The application is not fully offline on first run. It downloads model weights
once and caches them locally:

  * Face detection and face matching models, from the public OpenCV model zoo
    on GitHub.
  * A noise-suppression model, from a public GitHub repository.
  * A speech-separation model, from the Hugging Face model hub.

These are ordinary file downloads. No audio, video or personal data is sent
with them. As with visiting any website, the hosts serving those files can see
your IP address and that a download occurred. After the first run the files
are cached and no further downloads happen.

Apart from these one-time downloads, the application makes no outbound network
connections unless you explicitly enable the optional cloud feature in 13.4.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. RECORDING OTHER PEOPLE - PLEASE READ
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the part that matters most, and it is not a technical control.

One Voice points a camera and a microphone at people who are not you. It can
isolate one person's voice from a group, which means it can make an individual
audible and intelligible in a situation where they would otherwise be lost in
background noise. That is the entire purpose of the software, and it is also
its main privacy risk.

  * Tell people when the camera and microphone are on. Do not run a session
    covertly. The recording indicator exists to support this, not to replace
    telling someone.
  * Ask before you record. Recording consent laws differ by country and, in
    the United States, by state. Some jurisdictions require the consent of
    every person recorded, not just one.
  * Do not use One Voice to listen in on conversations you are not part of.
    The software cannot tell the difference between a conversation you have
    joined and one you are eavesdropping on. Only you can.
  * Take particular care with children, patients, clients and anyone who
    cannot meaningfully consent.
  * If you share a recording, transcript or summary, remember it contains
    another person's words.

The software will not enforce any of this for you. It is stated here because
the responsibility is real and it sits with the person operating the device.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. SAFETY PASSTHROUGH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The application includes a local sound classifier intended to let alarms and
sirens through rather than suppressing them along with other sound. It runs on
your machine and sends nothing anywhere.

This is a prototype feature and has not been certified or tested to any safety
standard. Do not rely on One Voice to alert you to an emergency. Do not use it
in any situation where failing to hear a warning could cause harm, including
driving, cycling, or moving around live traffic, machinery or construction.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. CHILDREN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One Voice is not directed at children and collects no information from anyone,
including children. It should not be operated by a child unsupervised, both
because it requires a laptop-class GPU and because operating it responsibly
means understanding sections 6 and 10.

13. PLANNED FEATURES AND THEIR PRIVACY IMPACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The following are in development and may not be present in the version you
are using. Sections 13.1 to 13.3 keep processing on your machine. Section 13.4
does not.

If any of these are not shipped, the corresponding section will be removed
from this policy rather than left describing software that does not exist.

13.1  LIVE TRANSCRIPTION

      Speech-to-text applied to the isolated audio of the person you have
      selected, shown on screen as the conversation happens.

      Because it runs on the separated stream rather than the raw room audio,
      the transcript is of the selected speaker, not of everyone nearby.

      The speech-to-text model runs locally. Transcripts are not uploaded. A
      transcript is still a written record of what another person said: treat
      it with the same care as a recording, and see section 10 before sharing.

13.2  CONVERSATION SUMMARY

      A short summary of a conversation, generated from the transcript in
      13.1.

      A summary is derived from another person's words and carries the same
      obligations as the transcript it came from. Summaries are stored with
      the conversation they belong to, on your machine, and are deleted when
      you delete it.

13.3  AUTOMATIC SPEAKER LOCK

      When exactly one person is detected in frame, the application may select
      that person automatically instead of waiting for you to tap their face.

      This is a convenience, but it means isolation can begin without a
      deliberate selection. Nothing else changes: the same camera and
      microphone are already active, the same recording indicator applies, and
      nothing is saved unless you start a recording. You can always clear the
      selection manually.

13.4  CLOUD IMAGE UNDERSTANDING  -  DATA LEAVES YOUR DEVICE

      READ THIS SECTION BEFORE ENABLING THE FEATURE.

      This feature lets you capture a still image of what the camera is seeing
      and send it to a third-party cloud service (Google Gemini) to describe
      what is in the picture. The purpose is to answer questions a transcript
      cannot: if someone refers to an object you cannot identify, the image
      can be described in context.

      Unlike every other feature in this document, this one transmits data off
      your computer.

      What would be sent:
        * A still image of the camera view at the moment of capture. This may
          include the faces of anyone in frame, the room you are in,
          documents, screens, or anything else visible.
        * Optionally, accompanying text context.

      What this means:
        * The image leaves your device and is processed on servers operated by
          a third party, under that provider's terms and privacy policy, not
          this one.
        * We cannot control, retrieve or delete data once it has been sent.
        * Bystanders in frame have not agreed to this and will usually not
          know it happened.

      How it is handled:
        * The feature is OFF by default and must be deliberately enabled.
        * Enabling it requires explicit confirmation that you understand
          images will be sent to a third party.
        * Capture is a deliberate action each time. There is no continuous or
          background upload of video.
        * Audio is never sent to this service. It handles still images only.
        * No API credentials are embedded in the front-end website.

      Our recommendation: do not use this feature with other people in frame
      unless you have told them and they have agreed. If you only need to
      identify an object, point the camera at the object rather than at the
      room.

14. YOUR RIGHTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Because we never receive your data, there is nothing for us to disclose,
correct, export or delete on your behalf. Every file One Voice creates is on
your own computer, under your own control, and can be deleted by you at any
time. Deletion instructions are in section 8.

If you are in a jurisdiction granting rights of access, correction, erasure,
portability or objection, those rights are exercised against whoever holds the
data. For everything One Voice does locally, that is you.

The exception is anything you chose to send under section 13.4, which is held
by the third-party provider under their policy. Requests about that data must
go to them.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
15. THIRD-PARTY COMPONENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One Voice is built on open-source components, including Three.js for the
website, MediaPipe and OpenCV for face detection and matching, and published
research models for speech separation, noise suppression and speech-to-text.
Their licences and attributions are listed in the CREDITS and LICENSE files
distributed with the project.

These components run locally as part of the application. Using One Voice does
not create any account or relationship with the organisations that publish
them. The optional cloud service in section 13.4 is the sole exception and
involves a direct relationship between you and that provider.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
16. LIMITATIONS AND HONESTY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One Voice is a research prototype produced during a hackathon. It is not
audited, not certified and not production software. It requires a connected
laptop with a supported GPU and is not a self-contained wearable device.
Separation quality varies with distance, room acoustics and the number of
people speaking. Transcripts and summaries are machine-generated and will
contain errors; do not rely on them as an accurate record of what was said.

No claim is made here that has not been read directly from the source code or
measured, and planned work is labelled as planned.
